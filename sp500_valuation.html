What this answers. Not "is this dear versus its own history" — the pack already does that — but "is this dear versus other stocks, today". Those are different claims with very different evidence behind them: a 2016 CRSP/Compustat study puts cross-sectional value at 5.50%/yr gross long-short (1990–2014) against 1.25%/yr for the own-history version. Cheap is coloured green here because that is the direction the cross-sectional evidence points; the same green on the pack's own-history view is a far weaker claim.

One sector is bunched into a single quintile: Real Estate puts 25 of its 31 ranked names in Q5; Utilities puts 19 of its 31 ranked names in Q3. That one is a property of the metric, not of the market: REITs carry heavy depreciation and are valued on FFO/AFFO, so their GAAP earnings — and therefore their P/E — are structurally understated. Read REITs on the sector quintile only. This is the general reason the sector quintile sits beside the market one throughout.

Method. NTM P/E = price ÷ NTM consensus EPS, where NTM EPS blends the consensus FY1 and FY2 annual EPS by the time remaining in FY1: w = days to FY1 end / 365, NTM EPS = w·FY1 + (1−w)·FY2. The blend is not a refinement — fiscal years end in different months, so a raw FY1 multiple compares a company eleven months into its year against one that is one month in, and the ranking would inherit that error.
Sources. Consensus EPS: FMP /stable/analyst-estimates (annual). Price and market cap: FMP /stable/quote. Constituents, company names and GICS sectors: Coverage Manager's cached Wikipedia list. All refreshed by the same Friday job that builds the weekly pack.
What this is not. It is a snapshot of embedded expectations, not a return forecast and not a buy list. A low multiple is frequently correct pricing of a deteriorating business, and consensus EPS is a lagging object — a name that has just guided down can print a flatteringly low multiple until estimates catch up. Cross-sector comparison on P/E alone is unsound, which is why the sector quintile sits beside the market one.
